01

What happens when there's no equation to solve

Kepler's equation exists because two-body motion happens to have a closed-form solution. Most real dynamics — J2 oblateness, drag, third-body effects, anything SGP4-shaped — don't. The only general option is numerical integration: walk forward from a known state in small time steps, re-evaluating the force at every one. RK4 does that with four force evaluations per step (k1..k4), weighted and combined into the next state.

This page deliberately runs the same real orbital elements Kepler's walkthrough used, on the same hardware, so the two pages' numbers are directly comparable — see the benchmarks page for that comparison.

02

The force evaluation: 1/r³ without division or a square root

Every stage needs d/dt[x,y,vx,vy] = [vx, vy, −x/r³, −y/r³], and 1/r³ needs both an inverse and a square root — neither exists in CKKS. The fix is the same shape as Kepler's reciprocal: Newton-Raphson, multiply-only, this time for inverse square root: y ← y·(3−r²y²)/2, cubed afterward (y³=y²·y).

Three rounds get it to about 1e-12 relative error, starting from a fixed public guess y₀=1/7000 — a generic "LEO altitude" prior, not derived from this satellite's actual radius. It only has to be roughly right because Newton's method converges quadratically, and LEO orbital radius sits in a narrow, known band regardless of which satellite it is.

03

The bug: bootstrapping values that were too big

The first version of this script encrypted raw kilometre-scale positions directly. It ran step 1 fine, then step 2 threw this on decrypt:

RuntimeError: Decode(): The decryption failed because the approximation error is too high. Check the parameters.

CKKS bootstrapping's internal sine-based approximation of modular reduction is only accurate within a calibrated range. Nothing had rescaled r² (~4.7×10⁷ at km scale) into it. The fix: canonical units — divide every length by a distance unit (7000 km) and every time by a matching time unit, chosen so μ becomes exactly 1. Positions and r² become O(1), comfortably inside CKKS's happy range.

This is the same discipline Approach E already uses (SCALE_KM2 rescales distance² before continuing) — this script's first draft just skipped it, and hit a real traceback for it, not a hypothetical.

How much of the error is CKKS, and how much is just RK4 being coarse

Both error sources compound step to step — each step's small error becomes part of the next step's starting state. At 4 steps, RK4's own coarseness (1.9 km by step 4) already dominates; matching Kepler's sub-meter floor needs about 180 much smaller steps (18_rk4_plaintext_reference.py's own error-vs-stepcount table), which is what makes the extrapolation on the benchmarks page necessary rather than just running it out.

Ring 213, HEStd_NotSet — demo, insecure parameters, same disclosure as the rest of this project. Measured on an Apple M-series chip (8 cores, macOS/arm64), OpenFHE built from source. Per-step decryption here was diagnostic, to measure and verify each step — a real deployment would carry ciphertext through all 4 steps and decrypt only the final result.

What this costs, next to the alternative

RK4 is the general-purpose fallback: it works for dynamics that have no closed form, and this page shows it actually running, genuinely encrypted, with a real bug fixed along the way. What it costs compared to Kepler's closed form — and what the accurate, 180-step version would cost, extrapolated honestly from this measurement — is on the benchmarks page.
